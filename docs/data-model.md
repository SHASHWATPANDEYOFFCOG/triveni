every number below was computed from the committed dataset, not typed by hand._

## The shape of the problem

A merchant on a payment gateway receives **one netted settlement per day, not one
line per sale**. That is what makes this a three-way problem rather than a lookup:

- **seed (committed)** - 520 rows, 32 settlement groups, 17 of them many-to-one, largest 14 payments into one credit.
- **full** - 5481 rows, 158 settlement groups, 47 of them many-to-one, largest 55 payments into one credit.

Plus, on every settlement: **18% GST on the MDR** (not on the sale - the classic
expensive mistake), **0.1% TDS under section 194-O** on gross, a **T+2** cycle over
the Indian bank calendar (2nd and 4th Saturdays closed), refunds netted into the
same cycle, chargeback holds, and a 5% rolling reserve released five business days
later.

## Integrity properties, enforced by tests

- Every truth group satisfies `gross + Sum(components) == net_expected`, to the paise.
  The generator refuses to emit a dataset where this fails.
- No underscore-prefixed generator hints reach the shipped source files. A real
  gateway response does not tell you which settlement a payment landed in.
- Bank `row_id`s are opaque (`TR` + 10 hex). Semantic ids like `unknown-0` would let
  a trivial 'matcher' read the prefix and score perfectly.
- `recon/`, `ingest/`, `forecast/` and `qa/` cannot import the labels - asserted by a test.
- Generation is byte-deterministic: same spec, same files.
